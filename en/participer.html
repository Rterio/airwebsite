Industry clinical trials

Our team also takes part in several clinical trials run by the pharmaceutical industry, testing new treatments for asthma, chronic cough and some ear, nose and throat conditions. Our team can tell you whether one of them might suit you.

How to take part

The main steps, from first contact to the end of your participation

  1. You express your interest

    Through the contact form, choosing "Taking part in a study". Just tell us how to reach you. No commitment on your part.

  2. Our team calls you back

    A team member asks you a few questions, confidentially, to see which current projects you could take part in.

  3. An information meeting

    If a project suits you, we walk you through it and answer your questions. You sign the consent form only if you wish, and you can take time to think it over and talk with your family.

  4. Study visits

    Visits take place at the IUCPQ and, depending on the project, include breathing tests, blood tests or questionnaires. The team is with you at every visit.

  5. End of your participation

    We thank you for your contribution, and you can receive a summary of the results once the project is complete.

Frequently asked questions

Can I choose which study I take part in?

No. To respect the ethics rules that govern our work, our team checks with you which projects you're eligible for. You remain completely free to accept or decline what is offered.

Can I withdraw from a study?

Yes, at any time, without having to give a reason. Your decision will have no effect on the care you receive at the IUCPQ.

Is my information kept confidential?

Yes. Your data are coded: your name does not appear in analysis files, and only authorized team members can access them. Each study's consent form explains how your information is protected.

Are the studies supervised?

Every study is approved by a research ethics committee before it begins. The committee ensures participants' safety, dignity and rights are protected.

Do I have to stop my medications?

Never change your treatment without advice from your doctor or the research team. If a study requires an adjustment, the team explains and supervises it.
